# Endzone Sim audit: coaching, matchup history, and flaws (Week 3, 2026-09-25)

Everything here was measured from nflverse data (play-by-play 2012–2026, `games.csv` with head coaches and closing
lines since 1999). The scripts are in this folder. Run them from a directory holding the downloaded data; the `gm/`
scripts go in `src/gm/` next to the model's own `gm/` code. Findings are ranked by how much they affect the bets you
make.

## 1. Live bug-level issue: touchdown-location defense adjustments are pure noise

The page runs the engine with `opp:1` (`app.js` `ctx()`). That applies two defense inputs straight to touchdown odds:

- `oppRZ = tdpos[pos]^0.5` multiplies each player's red-zone target weight by where that defense allowed passing TDs
  (RB/WR/TE).
- `rzRunLean = (1 - rtd) * 0.25` shifts red-zone run/pass mix by the defense's rush-TD share.

How much of each is real (2016–25, 320 team-seasons, `dvp.py`):

| Defense metric | split-half r | season reliability | year→year r |
|---|---|---|---|
| passing-TD share to RB | −0.06 | ≈0 | −0.03 |
| passing-TD share to WR | +0.04 | 0.07 | +0.03 |
| passing-TD share to TE | −0.08 | ≈0 | +0.03 |
| rush-TD share allowed | +0.03 | 0.07 | +0.09 |
| (for contrast) target share to RB | +0.28 | 0.44 | +0.27 |
| (for contrast) yards/carry allowed | +0.31 | 0.47 | +0.15 |

The TD-location numbers have no signal at all. This week they still move red-zone target weights by 0.81× to 1.24×. The
largest are DET RBs vs NYJ ×1.24, LA RBs vs DEN ×1.21, KC RBs vs MIA ×1.21, NYJ RBs vs DET ×0.81 and BUF RBs vs LAC ×0.85.
The Model tab already lists "defense-versus-position" under *tested and rejected*.

**Fix:** set `oppRZ` to 1 and `rzRunLean` to 0 (keep `tgt`, `ypc` and `ypt`, which do carry signal). Then re-run
`precompute.js` and the TD backtests, because `TDFIT` was fitted with these inputs on. Damage is limited because the model
gets only 0.32 weight in the final TD price.

## 2. Coaching changes: where they matter and where they don't

Seven teams have a new head coach in 2026 per nflverse: BAL, CLE, LV, MIA, NYG, PIT, TEN. Six of this week's 15 games
include one.

**Matters: player usage priors (`nfl_build.py`).** This test predicts weeks 4–18 target/carry share from last season's
share and weeks 1–3 (`share.py`, 2016–25). The fitted weight is the best blend of the two.

| | same head coach | new head coach |
|---|---|---|
| target share: best weight on last season | 49% | 32% |
| carry share: best weight on last season | 37% | ≈0% |
| error using last season alone (targets) | 0.046 | 0.054 |

At Week 3 the builder gives last season's targets `0.25 × 17 = 4.25` game-weights against about 1.2 for this season's two
games. That's roughly 75–80% before shrinkage, for every team. `EZPRE=0.25` was tuned on all teams together. New-coach
teams need clearly less. **Fix:** scale `PRIOR_REC_EARLY` and `PRIOR_W` by about 0.6 for targets and about 0 for carries
when the head coach changed. Coordinator changes aren't in nflverse data, so the same logic applies to them, but it has to
come from a hand list.

**Matters: pass rate (`team_env`).** Neutral-situation pass rate is a stable coach trait: in-season reliability is 0.75,
and year→year r is 0.46 with the same coach but 0.19 with a new one. At Week 3 last season still supplies 28% of the
estimate (this season 22%, league prior 50%). **Fix:** drop the last-season term for new-coach teams.

**Matters a little: fourth downs and the TD/FG mix (`engine.js`).** The engine applies one league-wide 4th-down rule set
(`fourth.py`, 2024–26, non-late situations):

| spot | engine go rate | actual |
|---|---|---|
| 4th & 1, opponent half | 0.80 | 0.92 |
| 4th & 1, own 28–50 | 0.55 | 0.56 |
| 4th & 2, opponent half | 0.58 | 0.60 |
| 4th & 3, inside the 30 | 0.22 | 0.32 |
| goal-to-go ≤4 inside the 5 | 0.50 | 0.39 |
| everything else | 0 | 0.06 |

Team go rates in 4th & ≤3 spots ranged from 0.30 (ARI) to 0.84 (DET) in 2025. That is a real coach trait: season
reliability is 0.45. A team's TD share of its scores (TD / (TD+FG)) has a smaller real component (true SD ≈ 0.05 around
0.59, reliability 0.41), and +10 points of go rate is worth about +1.4 points of TD share. Because each team's points are
pinned to DraftKings' total, the model gives every team the same TD/FG mix. A team one SD above average scores about 4%
more touchdowns at the same point total. This week's extremes meet in LAC @ BUF: BUF's 2025 TD share was 0.76 with a 0.83
go rate, LAC's was 0.48 with 0.43. **Fix (small):** raise 4th & 1 in plus territory to about 0.90. Optionally add a
shrunk per-team TD-share multiplier (about half the observed deviation).

**Doesn't matter: head-coach change and team ratings.** Last season's point differential predicts weeks 1–8 margin
equally well with a new coach (slope 0.425 vs 0.426), and the market prices both correctly (`coach.py`). The game model's
new-coach games early in the season are no worse than others (`gm/disagree.py`). Don't add a coaching-reset term to the
game model.

## 3. Historical matchups (team and player): no signal, keep them out

Tested on every game since 1999 (`h2h.py`), against the closing spread:

| predictor | games | correlation with this game's cover margin | "back last meeting's winner" covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| team vs team, last meeting | 6,813 | −0.002 (±0.024) | 50.1% |
| team vs team, last 3 meetings | 5,821 | +0.022 (±0.026) | 50.7% |
| same-season division rematch | 1,524 | +0.018 (±0.050) | 48.8% |
| head coach vs head coach, last meeting | 3,955 | −0.010 (±0.031) | 50.1% |
| coach vs coach, last 3 meetings | 1,726 | −0.008 (±0.047) | 49.8% |

The regression coefficient on the last meeting after controlling for the spread is −0.005.

**Player vs opponent** (`player_h2h.py`, 2012–25). The baseline is the player's own average over his previous 8 games.
The test asks whether beating or missing that baseline against this opponent before predicts beating it again. "Top/bottom
20%" are players whose past games vs this opponent were furthest above or below their baseline.

| stat | vs this defense (≥1 prior / ≥3 prior) | vs this opposing head coach (≥1 / ≥3) | top-20% vs bottom-20% history, beat baseline again |
|---|---|---|---|
| receiving yards | −0.010 / −0.003 (n=21,586 / 8,459) | −0.009 / +0.006 | 40.7% vs 41.9% |
| receptions | −0.009 / +0.001 | −0.008 / −0.002 | 42.3% vs 44.0% |
| rushing yards | −0.015 / −0.022 | −0.001 / +0.031 | 36.9% vs 39.4% |
| anytime TD | −0.011 / −0.034 | −0.012 / −0.014 | 31.2% vs 30.5% |

Every correlation is inside its 95% noise band (about ±0.013 at n=21,586). "He always goes off against them" has no
predictive value, and neither does "he struggles against that coach's defense". Rates below 50% reflect skew (the mean
sits above the median), so compare the top and bottom groups with each other.

**Team offense vs a specific defense** (`team_h2h.py`, 1999–2025), measured as points against the implied team total:
correlation +0.003 (n=13,626) vs the same defense and −0.013 vs the same opposing head coach. Teams that beat their total
by 4+ against an opponent before went over 49.3% the next time; teams that missed by 4+ went over 48.7%.

Matchup history (team vs team, coach vs coach, player vs team, player vs coach, offense vs defense) is noise once the
line and the player's recent form are known. The model is right not to use it.

## 4. Game model (moneyline/spread): it's mostly a QB model

Rebuilt from the artifact source; it reproduces the slate's margins exactly (`gm/decomp.py`). Fitted weights for Week 3:
`hfa 2.32, epa −8.6, pepa −6.9, repa +3.4, sr +21.4, qb +41.1, qbd −1.6`. The team-efficiency terms largely cancel. This
week they contribute −2.6 to +2.1 points while the QB term contributes up to ±9.3. Team quality outside the QB, defense
especially, barely registers. That's why SEA @ WAS comes out as WAS by 2.8 against DraftKings' SEA by 7.5: the model rates
Seattle's roster only 1.0 point better than Washington's.

What I tested and ruled out (walk-forward 2016–25, `gm/fixes.py`):

- Simpler efficiency features: MAE 10.30–10.32 vs 10.29 live (close 9.79). No gain, so the cancelling weights aren't
  a bug. The team ratings just add little.
- Home field: the intercept (2.2–2.4 since 2021) leaves home residuals near zero in 2021–25 (−0.21, +0.33, +0.69, −0.24,
  +0.13). Fine.
- Large disagreements aren't worse. When the model was 6+ points off the close its side covered 55.3% (347 games, about
  2 SE). That is weak evidence the other way, not a reason to fade them.

Found:

- **Second-year QBs are underrated** by about a point: the team beats the model by +1.14 ± 0.54 in a QB's second
  season. The model treats a rookie year as fixed ability. This week that's TEN's Cam Ward (−0.125 EPA/dropback, rookie
  year). The model has NYG by 7.5 and DraftKings has NYG by 2.5, so the model is probably about a point too far toward NYG.
- **QB ratings don't age or account for injury.** Deshaun Watson (+0.001, built partly from old Houston seasons) drives
  the model's CLE by 5.4 where DraftKings has CAR by 2.5. Rodgers is similar. Not testable here; treat big QB-driven gaps with stale or aging
  QBs skeptically.

The report card stays as it was: no edge on moneyline/spread.

## 5. Smaller items

- `gen_nfl.py` `pid_of(name)` never passes the team, so a duplicate name silently picks the last roster row. There are
  no collisions in this week's lists, but check whenever a common name (e.g. a second "Josh Allen") is added to OUT,
  QUESTIONABLE or STARTER.
- `bt_run.js` defaults to `scheme:'data'`; the live page runs `scheme:'off'`. Confirm that every graded run behind the
  report card set `EZV='{"scheme":"off"}'`. If any didn't, those grades describe a slightly different model than the
  live one.
- Defense profiles (`DefBook`) give last season 0.35 weight per play, about 75% of team-specific information at Week 3,
  with no regard for defensive-coordinator changes. The scheme table itself notes new DCs at NYJ and PIT. This has low
  impact while the coverage layer is off, but the same new-coach logic from §2 applies.

## Priority

1. Turn off `oppRZ` / `rzRunLean` and re-fit `TDFIT`. This directly affects anytime-TD prices, costs nothing, and the
   evidence is unambiguous.
2. Cut last-season usage and pass-rate weight for new-coach teams. This affects yards/catches, the one market with a
   positive record, and 6 of this week's 15 games.
3. Raise 4th & 1 in plus territory to about 0.90, and optionally add a team TD-share multiplier.
4. Don't add matchup history (team, player or coach level) or a coaching-change reset to team ratings.

2023–24 anytime TD vs 8,697 DraftKings prices (fit one season, test the other), and 2024–26 DraftKings props
(pregame actives, each season's role correction fitted without it).

| Change | Result | Kept? |

Honest read: the game model is clearly better at seeing team strength, but nothing here produced a proven edge
against DraftKings. The TD and prop changes are within noise of v3.3; they are kept because each removes a known
error (noise inputs, stale 4th-down behaviour) without costing accuracy.

## Spread and moneyline: what was tried to make picks significantly better (2026-09-25, later)

The goal was significantly better spread and moneyline picks at the prices available right before kickoff (the
close). Scripts are in `audit/gm2/`, all walk-forward (train on earlier seasons only).

| Approach | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Bet DraftKings when its price is off the rest of the market (`offmarket.py`) | 2021–25, ~20 books, same spread point | DK spreads were off-market in 2 of ~1,250 games; off-market moneylines lost (EV>0: n=111, +0.4% ± 15%) |
| Situational factors (`ats_data.py`, `ats_model.py`): rest, short week, bye, travel/time zones, west-coast early games, primetime, late season, wind, recent covers, last margin, QB change, home dog, big favourite | 2012–25, logistic and boosted trees | worse than a coin flip out of sample (log loss 0.697–0.701 vs 0.693) |
| Situational + game model features | 2018–25 | still worse than the game model's disagreement alone |
| Tuesday→close line movement, at the close | 2021–25 | steam side covers the close 49.7% (n=976) |
| Game model fitted directly to the closing line (`direct.py`) | 2016–25 | same as the current model |
| **Current game model's disagreement with the close** | 2016–25, 2,573 games | biggest 20% of disagreements cover 52.6% (+2.1% ± 4.3%), biggest 10% 53.5% (+3.9% ± 6.0%). Break-even at −110 is 52.4%. |

Conclusion: with public data, no version of the game model is significantly better than DraftKings' closing lines.
The one signal (the model's disagreement) is small and statistically unproven. Every extra input made it worse.

## Team ratings and line flags (the user's direction)

Rather than trying to beat the book everywhere, every team now carries a value (points vs an average team on a
neutral field, ranked 1–32) and a game is flagged when DraftKings' spread sits 3+ points from the ratings' fair line.
Record of such flags, closing lines 2016–25, each season rated from earlier seasons only (`gm/flag_record.py`):

market strength rating is in (10.03 → 10.04).
